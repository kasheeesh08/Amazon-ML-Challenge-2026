1. Project overview

1.1 Problem in simple terms

  • There are three independent sources of business records (name, address, country) with no shared identifiers.
  • Source 1 (S1) is the deduplicated reference list. S2 and S3 are noisy sources that are not internally deduplicated: one business can appear several times in each.
  • Task: for every S1 entity in test, list all S2/S3 records that are the same real-world business. That can be zero, one or many records (training maximum: 11 per S1).
  • Matching goes strictly S1 → {S2, S3}; outputs contain only S2-/S3- IDs. It must be an ML solution trained only on the provided data.

Sources: docs/problem_statement.pdf, and docs/COMPETITION_NOTES.md §1–§11, which carries page references to the PDF.

1.3 Metric: macro F0.5

  • Per S1 entity: P = |pred ∩ true| / |pred|, R = |pred ∩ true| / |true|, and F0.5 = 1.25·P·R / (0.25·P + R). The score is the average over all S1 entities, singletons included.
  • Singletons (no true match): an empty prediction scores 1.0; any prediction scores 0.0. 5.585% of training S1 are singletons.
  • β = 0.5 weights precision about 4× more than recall. One false merge on a singleton wipes out that entity's whole score, which is why the pipeline uses a conservative threshold and the one-per-target rule.
  • Two cases are UNRESOLVED in the official rules: true ≠ ∅ with an empty prediction, and true ≠ ∅ with no overlap. Both are scored 0.0 locally (R = 0), in src/ber/metrics.py; see COMPETITION_NOTES §4.
  • Leaderboard: the public score uses a subset of test; the final ranking uses the private remainder. The split size is UNRESOLVED.

1.4 Constraints (official materials, via COMPETITION_NOTES §5–§7)

  • External data is strictly prohibited. That includes external databases, APIs, entity-resolution services, government registries, geocoding, and data augmentation from the internet. Evidence of external lookup means disqualification.
  • Model licence and size: the final model must be MIT/Apache-2.0 and have at most 8B parameters. Our matcher is a GBDT trained from scratch, and no pretrained weights are used anywhere.
  • Format: an invalid format is not scored. Missing S1 rows, duplicate rows, duplicate IDs, S1 self-matches and non-existent IDs are rejected.

3. Step 1: validation split and local scorer

3.1 How the split was built

Source: src/build_validation_split.py and src/ber/validation_split.py; report experiments/validation_split/VALIDATION_SPLIT.md.

  • The unit of splitting is the S1 entity, not the pair: 1/5 of S1 go to validation, with seed 20260925.
  • Stratification: country × min(#true matches, 8) × (has an S2 match, has an S3 match), giving 38 non-empty strata. The largest per-stratum deviation from exactly 20% is 0.60 entities.
  • Order within each stratum: blake2b("<seed>:<entity_id>"). Quotas use floor plus largest remainder.
  • Leakage prevention:
    • Each S2/S3 target belongs to exactly one S1, re-checked at build time, and inherits its S1's fold.
    • Distractors are split 20/80, stratified by source × country.
    • So every record is in exactly one fold, and the fit and validation pools are disjoint. Verification found 0 fit targets inside the validation pool and 0 S1 in both folds.
  • Validation candidate pool: all targets of the validation S1 plus their share of the distractors, giving 2,064,020 S2+S3 records (4.676 per S1; full train 4.677). It reproduces the train composition, not test's 5.75 per S1. The report lists this as a known limitation: validation may be optimistic on precision.
  • The test files are never read by Step 1.

4. Step 2: blocking and candidate generation

Key principle: a TRUE MATCH must enter the candidate set, otherwise the matcher can never recover it. Candidate recall is therefore a hard ceiling on end-to-end recall. Every candidate is also a chance for a false merge, which matters a lot under F0.5.

4.1 Normalisation (src/ber/normalize.py, NORM_VERSION 2, identical to the EDA)

  • General: NFKC, casefold, Latin diacritics stripped, punctuation turned into spaces, & → and.
  • Name keys:
    • name_raw: the stripped raw name.
    • name_n1: the basic-normalised name.
    • name_n2: name_n1 with runs of single letters joined and legal-form tokens removed (inc, llc, ltd, pvt, private, corp, sarl, sas, …, plus some Devanagari forms).
    • name_n3: the tokens of name_n2, sorted, so word order doesn't matter.
    • name_tok1: the first token of name_n2.
  • Address keys:
    • addr_n1: the normalised comma-separated components.
    • addr_n2: the sorted set of address tokens, with street and state abbreviations expanded (Rd → road, NC → north carolina).
    • addr_digits: the sorted set of digit groups, with leading zeros removed.
    • postal: a 5- or 6-digit code.
    • house: the house number, with leading zeros stripped.
    • country: casefolded.
  • Flags: name missing, address missing, non-Latin name, domain-like name, junk prefix, postal present, landmark.
  • Retrieval tokens:
    • name: the words of name_n2 plus character trigrams of the joined name
    • address: alphabetic tokens, digit groups and the house number

5.2 Model (src/ber/gbdt.py)

  • A histogram GBDT written from scratch in numba (no GBDT library was available), with binary log-loss.
  • Settings:
    • 300 trees, depth 6, learning rate 0.1
    • L2 λ = 1.0, minimum child hessian 20
    • 64 quantile bins, with edges taken from a 400k-row seeded sample
    • seed 0
  • Deterministic: no row or feature subsampling.
  • Step 3 training data (leakage-safe): an internal set A of 441,364 fit S1 with a validation-sized pool of their targets plus fit distractors.
    • A1 (353,091 S1) is used for fitting; A2 (88,273 S1) is used only for selection.
    • Rows: all 1,200,294 positives plus a deterministic 10% hash sample of negatives (1,942,608) with weight 10.
  • Selection maximised the official macro F0.5 on A2, never on validation. The candidates were logistic regression and GBDT at 50/100/150/200/300 trees, each with and without the one-to-one rule. Chosen: GBDT, 300 trees, one-to-one ON, threshold 0.69 (A2 F0.5 0.96289).
  • Gain importance: t_rank 76.5%, addr_cont_t 9.1%, t_gap 6.1%, nameaddr_cos 1.6%, and all others below 1%.

5.3 Decision rule (submission_package/.../src/config/matcher_config.json)

  1. Predict a match if probability ≥ 0.69.
  2. One-S1-per-target: if several S1 claim the same S2/S3 record, only the highest-probability S1 keeps it. This is justified because no training target belongs to more than one S1.
  3. No other post-processing.

8. Test-scale and stress evaluation

8.1 Why and how

  • Why: the test pool is about 5× the validation pool and has a different S1-to-pool ratio. The model's target-context features depend on which S1 compete for each target.
  • Unlabelled test check (experiments/test_scale/TEST_SCALE_REPORT.md): the Step 3 model was run on test, counts only.
    • 126,274,337 candidates.
    • Test S1 per pool record is 0.1738, against 0.2138 on validation.
    • Target-context feature drift is negligible: PSI t_rank 0.0297, t_ncand 0.0709, t_gap 0.0719. n_cand_all shows moderate drift (0.22).
  • Labelled stress evaluation (experiments/TEST_SCALE_REPORT.md, experiments/stress_matcher_eval.py): the same 441,364 validation S1 matched against the full training pool (10,320,219 records).
    • The 8.26M added fit-fold records are true non-matches, so the labels stay complete.
    • This setup has 0.043 S1 per pool record, far more "orphaned" than test's 0.174.

8.3 Conclusions

Justified:

  • The matcher is stable when each target's owner is among the queries.
  • It is not robust when owners are missing: it makes confident false merges, especially on legal-form near-duplicates.
  • The GBDT relies heavily on t_rank.

NOT justified:

  • That test F0.5 will be 0.840. B is a pessimistic environment, not a replica of test.
  • Anything about France. There are no labelled France records.

9.3 What the diagnostic supports

All of the following are measured, on single deterministic runs with no repeated seeds or confidence intervals:

  • t_rank alone is not the cause. B, C and D stay within ±0.004 of A in every environment. When t_rank is removed, the model moves its reliance to t_gap.
  • The target-context family as a whole is orphan-sensitive. Removing all three (B2) raises S by 0.0615 but lowers V by 0.0076 and T by 0.0031. At the test-like ratio these features still add value.
  • In S the damage is mostly overconfident probabilities. Model A at threshold 0.95 reaches 0.9117 in S, close to B2's best of about 0.916. The best threshold rises with orphan density: about 0.71 in V, 0.77 in T and 0.96 in S.
  • At the test-like ratio, the orphan effect costs about 0.009 (V 0.9628 → T 0.9543). The rest of the gap to the leaderboard score is UNRESOLVED.

Caveats:

  • T assumes the extra test records resemble random fit-fold records. Their true nature is UNRESOLVED.
  • No statistical significance testing was done. Differences below about 0.001 should be treated as noise-level. This is a judgement, not a measured value.

12. What we currently know

A. Experimentally confirmed (measured)

  1. The GBDT beats logistic regression on validation (0.96285 vs 0.94458). 300 trees beat 50–200, and the one-to-one rule helps slightly (A2).
  2. Blocking recall is 98.214% on validation. It falls to 95.631% against a test-sized pool; the ceiling drops as the pool grows.
  3. Legal-form, casing, word-order and address-reorder variants are 100% recalled by blocking. Native-script names (89.26%) and empty target addresses (90.02%) are the weakest blocking cases.
  4. The stress environment drops F0.5 to 0.840, and 90% of its false positives are on fit-fold records (183,185 orphaned targets and 89,373 fit distractors).
  5. Restricted to validation-pool records, the stress predictions score 0.956.
  6. Removing, capping or taking the percentile of t_rank changes F0.5 by no more than about 0.004 in V, S or T.
  7. Removing all target-context features helps S (+0.062) and hurts V (−0.0076) and T (−0.0031).
  8. At the test S1-to-pool ratio (T) the current model scores 0.9543.
  9. The best threshold rises with orphan density (about 0.71 in V, 0.77 in T, 0.96 in S). In T, threshold 0.75–0.78 gives 0.9557–0.9559 against 0.9543 at 0.69.
  10. Target-context feature drift between validation and real test is negligible (PSI ≤ 0.072).
  11. Final model on test: 5,503,470 predictions and 6.40% zero-prediction S1. The validator passes.
  12. India is consistently weaker than the US: validation 0.9503 vs 0.9712, and every environment agrees.

B. Suspected but NOT proven (hypotheses)

  1. France (15% of test, no labels) causes a large part of the remaining gap of about 0.03. If the non-France 85% scored like T (0.954), France would need about 0.75 to give 0.924.
  2. France is over-merged. Final-model test statistics: France 3.518 predictions per S1 and 4.62% zero-prediction, against US/India 3.04–3.21 and 6.1–7.2%. For reference, the training singleton rate is 5.58%. This is suggestive only.
  3. The extra test pool records (5.75 vs 4.68 per S1) behave like orphans or distractors, lowering precision.
  4. The test candidate recall ceiling is nearer the full-pool figure (about 95.6%) than validation's 98.2%.
  5. Validation is optimistic on precision, because it reproduces the train pool composition (VALIDATION_SPLIT.md).
  6. Near-duplicates that differ only in legal form are intentionally distinct entities in the data generator, so name-similarity features alone cannot separate them.

C. Not tested yet

  1. Leave-one-country-out (train US, evaluate India, and the reverse) as a proxy for unseen-country robustness.
  2. Orphan-aware training, i.e. training with fit-fold orphans added to the internal training pool.
  3. Any threshold other than 0.69 on the leaderboard. No alternative model or threshold has been submitted.
  4. GBDT hyperparameters other than tree count: depth, learning rate, λ, minimum child weight and bins were never tuned.
  5. Transliteration or script-aware handling of native-script names, in blocking or features.
  6. Special handling of empty-address targets.
  7. The end-to-end F0.5 effect of larger retrieval K, or an extra name channel. Only candidate recall was measured.
  8. Pretrained or embedding models (allowed if MIT/Apache and ≤ 8B; none used).
  9. A full-scale run of the packaged run_pipeline.py.
  10. Statistical significance or seed variance for any comparison.
