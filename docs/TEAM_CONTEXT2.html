Evidence labels used throughout:
  • A measured local validation, labelled
  • B measured test-like or stress environment, labelled
  • C unlabelled real-test observation
  • D actual leaderboard score
  • E hypothesis or estimate
An E number is never a measured leaderboard result.
Labelled environments used by the diagnostics (all use the 441,364 validation S1 and their labels):
  • V: normal validation (validation pool, 4.68 pool records per S1).
  • T: test-ratio pool (validation pool plus random fit-fold records, 0.174 S1 per pool record, the same as the real test). Its target-context profile matches the real test.
  • S: full 10.3M training pool, test-sized but orphan-heavy (0.043 S1 per pool record). Blocking on the real test behaves like S.
"Step 3 model" means experiments/matching/model_gbdt.npz. The submitted final model (experiments/final/model_final.npz) was trained on all labels, so it cannot be scored on labelled data.

PHASE 2 — Completed diagnostics (all read-only; production untouched)

1. t_rank diagnostic CLOSED

  • Question: is t_rank (76.5% of GBDT gain) behind the gap?
  • Experiment: five variants retrained on the internal split (diagnostic only; no production retrain or inference):
    • A: current model
    • B: without t_rank
    • B2: without all three target-context features
    • C: t_rank capped at 1
    • D: percentile t_rank
    Each was evaluated on V, T and S. The test-ratio environment T was introduced here.
  • Results:
    • B V / T / S for A: 0.96285 / 0.95432 / 0.84002.
    • B, C and D stay within ±0.004 of A everywhere; t_gap takes over from t_rank.
    • B2: 0.95527 / 0.95125 / 0.90154.
    • The best threshold rises with orphan density (about 0.71 in V, 0.77 in T, 0.96 in S).
  • Conclusion: t_rank alone is not the cause. The target-context family is orphan-sensitive but still net-positive at the test ratio. experiments/diagnostics/trank/TRANK_DIAGNOSTIC_REPORT.md

2. Leave-one-country-out (LOCO) CLOSED

  • Question: how badly does the matcher degrade in an unseen country, as France is?
  • Experiment: US-only, India-only and size-matched US-only models, trained with the Step 3 protocol (diagnostic retraining, internal set only), evaluated per country on V, T and S.
  • Results A/B:
    • India → US: −0.011 on V and −0.015 on T. It over-merges: false positives double, singleton F0.5 drops about 10 points, and the best threshold moves up.
    • US → India: −0.078 on V and −0.076 on T. Recall collapses (79% vs 92%).
    • Per-country models gain at most about 0.001 in-country.
  • France, unlabelled C:
    • Fewest S1 with no match (4.62% vs US 6.13%, India 7.20%).
    • Generic names are not left empty more often.
    • Very generic names (e.g. "Bordeaux Club SAS" ×516).
  • Conclusion: unseen-country transfer is real but asymmetric. France's predictions show the over-merge pattern, which is suggestive only. experiments/diagnostics/leave_one_country_out/LOCO_REPORT.md

3. France-only threshold CLOSED

  • Question: would a higher threshold for France only help?
  • Experiment: filter the submitted predictions for France S1 at 0.80, 0.82, 0.85 and 0.88. No retraining or inference.
  • Results:
    • C 0.80 removes 23,683 France predictions (2.59%), and France S1 with no match go from 4.62% to 4.96%. 0.88 removes 48,481 (5.31%).
    • A/B In the labelled analogues, 54–85% of removed predictions were true matches.
    • E Overall effect about ±0.001.
  • Conclusion: a weak lever; do not pursue. experiments/diagnostics/france_threshold/FRANCE_THRESHOLD_REPORT.md

4. Target-context / t_rank ablation CLOSED

  • Question: does removing or neutralising the target-context features fix the shift?
  • Experiment: reused the t_rank diagnostic's models; added entity metrics and feature distributions, including the real test. No retraining or inference.
  • Results:
    • B Validation-to-stress gap: A 0.1228; without t_rank 0.1253; without all target context 0.0537. That comes at −0.0076 on V and −0.0031 on T.
    • C Real-test target-context distributions are close to V and T (PSI ≤ 0.072), far from S (0.56–1.17).
    • 99.8% of true positives have t_rank = 0: it works as a soft "best claimant" rule.
  • Conclusion: keep the current matcher. experiments/diagnostics/target_context_ablation/TARGET_CONTEXT_ABLATION_REPORT.md

5. Blocking-gap investigation CLOSED

  • Question: could candidate generation explain the gap?
  • Experiment: analysis of saved candidate sets plus a hybrid estimate. No retraining or inference.
  • Results:
    • C The real test's candidates per S1 (mean 72.9, p99 255) match S, not T. Blocking depends on absolute pool size.
    • B A perfect matcher on the candidates would score: V 0.99386, T 0.99305, S 0.98453.
    • Test-sized blocking costs −0.0063 (T 0.9543 → hybrid 0.9480).
    • Weakest categories at test size: non-Latin 80.8%, empty address 78.5%, house number differs 88.9%, generic names (India 88.1%).
  • Conclusion: blocking is healthy but costs about 0.006; do not modify. experiments/diagnostics/blocking_gap/BLOCKING_GAP_REPORT.md

6. Deep France error analysis CLOSED

  • Question: which France-like error patterns could explain the gap?
  • Experiment: analogue profiling, France-profile reweighting, and a category-mix precision estimator validated on the LOCO cases. No retraining or inference.
  • Results:
    • The closest analogue is US in T.
    • C France-specific traits:
      • 4.2% of France S1 share a name with 100+ others
      • initialism names ("SC") make up 1.74% of the pool, 9–17× the other countries
      • few house-number conflicts
    • The implied precision of France's category mix is 0.969, no worse than the US.
    • Fewer S1 with no match is a test-wide pattern.
  • Classification: D, unresolvable, with weak signs of over-merging.
  • Conclusion: no France-specific change is justified. experiments/diagnostics/france_error_analysis/FRANCE_ERROR_ANALYSIS_REPORT.md

7. Orphan-aware training CLOSED

  • Question: does adding orphaned targets to training improve robustness at the test ratio?
  • Experiment: internal set A plus 475,494 fit-fold orphans, bringing the pool to 5.754 per S1; retrained with the Step 3 protocol (diagnostic only, not production). The threshold chosen on A2 was 0.69.
  • Results B:
    • V: 0.96172 (−0.0011). T: 0.95652 (+0.0022). S: 0.87194 (+0.032).
    • At matched V, the T gain is only +0.0008.
    • Target-context gain falls from 82.8% to 55.1%.
  • Conclusion: most of the gain is just conservatism; not worth a submission. Stop this family. experiments/diagnostics/orphan_aware_training/ORPHAN_AWARE_TRAINING_REPORT.md

8. Train-vs-test distribution audit CLOSED

  • Question: is the gap a population difference?
  • Experiment: distribution tables, a test-matched labelled subset and singleton calibration. No retraining or inference.
  • Results:
    • C S1 shares: US 60%/India 40% in train vs US 38.3%/India 46.8%/France 15.0% in test. Pool per S1 is +23–24% (test 5.53–5.82 vs 4.67–4.68). Source mix and missing addresses are not shifted.
    • C The largest new shift does not depend on the model: only 0.07–0.31% of test S1 lack a candidate with cosine ≥ 0.5, against 1.8–3.2% in V/T. Either there are fewer singletons, or singletons have lookalikes.
    • C Under the Step 3 model, test has +7% predictions per S1 and roughly double the [0.69, 0.80) band.
    • B The test-matched subset scores 0.9523 in T and 0.9459 with blocking losses.
    • B Lookalike singletons in T: F0.5 0.854 (US) / 0.793 (India), against 0.894 / 0.854 for all singletons. E A cost of −0.002 to −0.009.
  • Conclusion: no single shift explains about 0.03. France would need about 0.80–0.87. experiments/diagnostics/test_distribution_audit/TEST_DISTRIBUTION_AUDIT_REPORT.md
